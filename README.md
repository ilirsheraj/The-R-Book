This is the code for **The R Book, Third Edition**, by Elinor Jones, Simon Harden, and Michael J. Crawley. I'm going through the book while running the code at the same time.

It's a very cool piece of work. It's massive—800+ pages of code, basic theory, statistical models, and explanations covering a huge variety of methods and approaches to problems from very diverse fields.

I wish I'd known about this book years ago when I was struggling to learn R and statistical modeling. I'd describe it as a relatively shallow but **extremely broad survey of applied statistical modeling**, with everything demonstrated in R. You won't come away an expert in every method it covers, but you get an impressive overview of the statistical toolbox: what kinds of problems exist, what methods are available to deal with them, and how those methods can actually be applied.

Looking back, I spent so much time years ago learning applications of some of these models in SPSS. I think having a resource like this—and learning the underlying statistical modeling alongside R—would have given me a much better foundation.
